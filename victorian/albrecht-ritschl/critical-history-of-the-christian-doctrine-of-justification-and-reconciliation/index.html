A Critical History of the Christian Doctrine of Justification and Reconciliation

18702 min read

Albrecht Ritschl — A Critical History of the Christian Doctrine of Justification and Reconciliation, translated from the German with the author’s sanction by John S. Black (Edinburgh: Edmonston and Douglas, 1872).

The first, historical volume of Ritschl’s Die christliche Lehre von der Rechtfertigung und Versöhnung (1870), the companion of the constructive third volume. Ritschl traces how the Church has understood the saving work of Christ: from Anselm and Abelard, through Thomas Aquinas and Duns Scotus and the mediaeval idea of justification, to the Reformers’ principle of justification by faith and their doctrine of reconciliation set against Osiander’s; then the orthodox Lutheran and Calvinist systems and the Socinian controversy, their disintegration under the theologians of the Illumination, Kant’s restatement of the problem, Schleiermacher and his followers, Pietism and the revived Lutheran orthodoxy, and the speculative school of Schelling, Hegel and their successors.
