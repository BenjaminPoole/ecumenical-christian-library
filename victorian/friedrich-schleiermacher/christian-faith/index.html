The Christian Faith

The Christian Faith

1821–1822; second edition 1830–18313 min read

Friedrich Schleiermacher — The Christian Faith, translated from the second German edition (1830–1831) and edited by H. R. Mackintosh and J. S. Stewart (Edinburgh: T. & T. Clark, 1928).

The great systematic theology of the father of modern liberal theology, in which doctrine is the church’s account of the Christian’s God-consciousness, “the feeling of absolute dependence”, as Christ has shaped it. After an introduction on the church, on Christianity and on the method of dogmatics, the first part develops the religious self-consciousness that every Christian experience presupposes (creation, preservation, and the divine attributes of eternity, omnipresence, omnipotence and omniscience); the second part sets out its two sides, the consciousness of sin (original and actual sin, the holiness and justice of God) and the consciousness of grace (the person and work of Christ, regeneration, sanctification, election, the church, the means of grace and the last things); and a conclusion treats the divine Trinity.
