Benjamin B. Warfield

Benjamin B. Warfield

Oct 12, 20263 min read

Benjamin Breckinridge Warfield (1851–1921), professor of didactic and polemic theology at Princeton Theological Seminary from 1887 until his death, the successor of Charles and A. A. Hodge and the last great theologian of Old Princeton. A scholar of the New Testament text before he was a dogmatician, he defended the inspiration and inerrancy of Scripture, the Westminster Standards and the Augustinian doctrine of grace against the liberalism and perfectionism of his day, in some thirty books and a great many articles and reviews.

Works (6)

  • Counterfeit Miracles — The Thomas Smyth Lectures for 1917–1918, delivered at Columbia Theological Seminary, South Carolina. Warfield argues that the miraculous gifts of the apostolic age were the credentials of the apostles and ceased with them, and then examines the claims to miracle that followed: the marvels of the Fathers and the Middle Ages, the miracles of Lourdes and the Roman Church, the tongues and prophecies of Edward Irving’s congregation, the faith-healing movement, and the mind-cure of Christian Science. (12 notes)
  • Faith and Life — Forty-one addresses given to the students at the Sunday afternoon conferences in the seminary’s Oratory, each on a text of Scripture: Elijah at Horeb and Old Testament religion, the wrath of man, childlikeness, the conviction and the outpouring of the Spirit, prayer, the witness of the Spirit to our sonship, the alien righteousness, peace with God, entire sanctification, the mystery of godliness, and God’s holiness and ours. (41 notes)
  • The Lord of Glory — Warfield surveys every title the New Testament gives Jesus — Jesus, Christ, Lord, Son of Man, Son of God — book by book, from Mark through the Apocalypse, to show that the conviction of His deity is not a late layer laid over the gospel story but the presupposition of every part of the New Testament, the Synoptic Gospels and their supposed primitive sources included. (14 notes)
  • The Plan of Salvation — Warfield sets out the possible answers to the question how God saves, as a series of choices, each narrowing the last: naturalism or supernaturalism (autosoterism against salvation from God), sacerdotalism or evangelicalism (the Church as the channel of grace, or God working immediately on the soul), universalism or particularism, and, within particularism, the order of the decrees — ending in Calvinism as evangelicalism consistently held. (6 notes)
  • The Power of God unto Salvation — Eight sermons preached in the chapel of the Theological Seminary at Princeton: on the revelation of man in Christ, the saving Christ, the argument from Christian experience, the paradox of omnipotence, the love of the Holy Ghost, the leading of the Spirit, Paul’s earliest gospel in 1 Thessalonians, and false religion and the true. (8 notes)
  • The Saviour of the World — Nine sermons preached to the students of Princeton Seminary, each opening with its text: the prodigal son, the one Name under heaven, the Lamb of God, God’s immeasurable love in John 3:16, the gospel of Paul, the glorified Christ, the risen Jesus, the gospel of the covenant, and the imitation of the incarnation in Philippians 2. (9 notes)
