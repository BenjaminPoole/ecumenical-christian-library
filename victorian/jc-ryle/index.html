J. C. Ryle

J. C. Ryle

Oct 25, 20262 min read

John Charles Ryle (1816–1900) was the first Bishop of Liverpool and the outstanding evangelical Anglican writer of the Victorian era. A Christ Church man and a fine athlete who lost the family fortune at twenty-five, he spent thirty-eight years in country parishes in Suffolk before Disraeli sent him to Liverpool at sixty-four. He wrote as he preached — plainly, directly, and for ordinary people.

All of Ryle’s works are in the public domain.

  • Texts that appear twice. Ryle reworked his own tracts into his books, so a few pieces are held here in two forms, under the title each was published with. The main ones: Thirst Relieved (Holiness) and Let Any Man Come (The Upper Room); The Good Way and the tract The Old Paths; Moses — An Example and the tract Faith’s Choice; Thoughts for Young Men and the tract Hints to Young Men.
