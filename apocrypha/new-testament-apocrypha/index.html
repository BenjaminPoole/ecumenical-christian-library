New Testament Apocrypha

New Testament Apocrypha

Nov 25, 20265 min readHereticalNew Testament Apocrypha

Gospels, acts, letters and apocalypses written in the centuries after the apostles, in the names of Christ’s first followers and outside the New Testament canon.

The translations are those of the Ante-Nicene Fathers, volumes 8 and 9, from New Advent, and M. R. James’s The Apocryphal New Testament (1924), all in the public domain. Entries run in rough order of composition.

Pilate and the Passion

  • Gospel of Nicodemus — Acts of Pilate (Greek A and B, Latin) with the Descent of Christ into Hell.
  • Report of Pilate — Pilate’s report to Caesar on Jesus’ miracles, crucifixion and resurrection, in two Greek forms.
  • Death of Pilate — Tiberius healed by Veronica’s image of Christ, and Pilate’s punishment and suicide.
  • Giving Up of Pilate — Pilate’s trial and condemnation before Caesar and his death (Paradosis Pilati).
  • Letter of Pilate to Claudius — Pilate’s letter to Claudius on the Jews, the miracles and resurrection of Jesus (from the Latin Gospel of Nicodemus).
  • Letter of Pilate to Tiberius — Short letter in which Pilate tells Tiberius of the crucifixion and signs.
  • Narrative of Joseph of Arimathaea — Joseph’s imprisonment, Christ’s appearance to him, and the two robbers.
  • Avenging of the Saviour — Titus healed by Veronica’s cloth; the destruction of Jerusalem and Pilate’s fate.

Gospels

  • Gospel of the Egyptians (fragments) — Sayings of Jesus to Salome on birth and the end, quoted by Clement of Alexandria.
  • Gospel of the Hebrews — Lost Jewish-Christian gospel known from patristic quotations (Clement, Origen, Jerome).
  • Kerygma Petri — Fragments of the Preaching of Peter, an early apology quoted by Clement.
  • Gospel of the Ebionites — Adoptionist harmony gospel of the Ebionites, quoted by Epiphanius.
  • Gospel of Peter — Fragmentary passion and resurrection gospel from the Akhmim codex.
  • Infancy Gospel of Thomas — Miracles and boyhood pranks of the child Jesus, in two Greek forms and a Latin form.
  • Protevangelium of James — Birth and childhood of Mary and the nativity of Jesus, told by James.
  • Gospel of Bartholomew — Questions of Bartholomew: the descent into Hades, the Virgin’s account and Satan’s confession.
  • Passing of Mary — Two Latin accounts of the death and assumption of the Virgin Mary.
  • Arabic Infancy Gospel — Miracles of the infant Jesus in Egypt and Nazareth, from a Syriac original.
  • History of Joseph the Carpenter — Jesus recounts the life and death of Joseph on the Mount of Olives.
  • Gospel of Pseudo-Matthew — Latin expansion of the Protevangelium and infancy stories, with the flight into Egypt.
  • Gospel of the Nativity of Mary — Abridged Latin account of Mary’s birth, youth and betrothal to Joseph.

Acts

  • The Acts of Paul and Thecla — Thecla of Iconium follows Paul, survives the fire and the beasts, and teaches.
  • Acts of Thomas — Thomas’s mission to India: the Hymn of the Pearl, thirteen acts and the martyrdom.
  • Acts of Andrew — Andrew’s miracles in Greece (Gregory of Tours’ epitome), prison discourses and crucifixion at Patras.
  • Acts of Andrew and Matthias — Andrew rescues Matthias from the man-eaters.
  • Acts of Philip — Philip’s travels at Athens and Hierapolis and his martyrdom.
  • Acts of Thaddaeus — Thaddaeus heals King Abgar of Edessa.
  • Doctrine of Addai — Syriac account of the mission of Addai (Thaddaeus) to Edessa, the healing of Abgar and the founding of the church there; fragmentary beginning.
  • Martyrdom of Andrew — Letter of the Achaian clergy on Andrew’s trial and crucifixion under Aegeates.
  • Acts of Barnabas — Journeyings and martyrdom of Barnabas in Cyprus, by ‘John Mark’.
  • Acts of Bartholomew — Bartholomew in India and his martyrdom (Martyrdom of Bartholomew).
  • Acts of John the Theologian — John’s exile to Patmos and his departure, ascribed to Prochorus.
  • Acts of Peter and Paul — Peter and Paul at Rome, their contest with Simon Magus and martyrdom under Nero.
  • Consummation of Thomas — Thomas’s imprisonment and martyrdom under King Misdeus.
  • The Acts of Peter and Andrew — Peter and Andrew in the city of the barbarians, and the camel through the needle’s eye.
  • The Life of Xanthippe, Polyxena and Rebecca — Romance of three Spanish women converted through Paul, Peter and Andrew.
  • Acts and Martyrdom of Matthew — Matthew’s mission to the Ethiopian king Fulbanus and his martyrdom.

Apocalypses

  • Apocalypse of Peter — Vision of heaven and hell given to Peter: Akhmim Greek fragment and the Ethiopic text.
  • Apocalypse of Paul — Paul is led by an angel through paradise and the torments of hell (Visio Pauli).
  • Apocalypse of Thomas — Latin prophecy of the seven signs before the end of the world; one gap marked […] where the scan is cut.
  • Apocryphal Apocalypse of John — John on Mount Tabor is shown the last things and the Antichrist.
  • The Apocalypse of the Virgin — Mary is shown the punishments of the damned and intercedes for them.

Letters

  • Epistula Apostolorum — The risen Christ’s revelations to the eleven apostles, written against Simon and Cerinthus.
  • Correspondence of Abgar and Jesus — King Abgar of Edessa asks Jesus to heal him, and Jesus promises to send a disciple; Thaddaeus later fulfils it.
  • Correspondence of Paul and Seneca — Fourteen forged Latin letters between the apostle Paul and the philosopher Seneca.
  • Epistle to the Laodiceans — Short Latin cento of Pauline phrases written to fill the gap of Col. 4:16.
  • Letter of Lentulus — Forged report to the Roman senate describing the physical appearance of Jesus.
