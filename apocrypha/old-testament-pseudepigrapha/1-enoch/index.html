1 Enoch

1 Enoch

c. 300 BC–AD 50 (five books of different dates)2 min readadapted from WikisourceHereticalOT Pseudepigrapha

Written: Jerusalem, c. 3rd century BC - 1st century AD. Open in Google Maps

1 Enoch, also called Ethiopic Enoch, is a collection of five Jewish apocalyptic books attributed to Enoch, the seventh from Adam (Genesis 5:18–24), and is wholly preserved only in Ge’ez (Ethiopic). It is divided here into its five traditional books, in R. H. Charles’s English translation (1917) of the Ethiopic text, which Charles divided into 108 chapters. Charles’s editorial signs for interpolated, corrupt, restored or supplied words (⌈ ⌉, † †, 〈 〉 and brackets) are kept as printed; his apparatus footnotes are omitted. Where a Greek or Latin version survives for a passage, Charles printed it alongside the Ethiopic, and that is kept.

  • Book of the Watchers (chapters 1–36) — the descent of the Watchers, the giants and the flood, and Enoch’s journeys through earth and Sheol.
  • Book of the Parables (chapters 37–71) — three parables on the Son of Man, the Elect One and the Lord of Spirits, with a Noah fragment.
  • Astronomical Book (chapters 72–82) — the courses of the sun, moon, stars and winds as shown to Enoch by Uriel.
  • Book of Dream Visions (chapters 83–90) — the Flood vision and the Animal Apocalypse, an allegorical history of Israel to the Maccabean age.
  • Epistle of Enoch (chapters 91–108) — the Apocalypse of Weeks, woes on the sinners, the birth of Noah and a closing promise to the righteous.
