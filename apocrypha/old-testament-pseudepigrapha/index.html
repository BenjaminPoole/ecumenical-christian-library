Old Testament Pseudepigrapha

Old Testament Pseudepigrapha

Nov 25, 20263 min readHereticalOT Pseudepigrapha

Jewish and early Christian writings issued in the names of Old Testament figures: apocalypses, testaments, retold scripture, psalms and legends.

Most come from R. H. Charles’s Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha of the Old Testament (1913) and the SPCK translations of the early twentieth century, with others from the Ante-Nicene Fathers; all are in the public domain, except that 4 Baruch is the Kraft and Purintun edition, freely available for non-commercial use with credit. Entries run in rough order of composition.

  • 1 Enoch — Ethiopic Enoch: five books on the Watchers, the Parables of the Son of Man, the heavenly luminaries, dream visions of history and the Epistle of Enoch (folder, 5 part notes plus contents note).
  • Letter of Aristeas — Legendary account of the translation of the Torah into Greek by seventy-two Jewish elders at the court of Ptolemy II.
  • Jubilees — Retelling of Genesis 1 to Exodus 12 as an angelic revelation to Moses, dated by jubilees (5 parts: Jubilees/Jubilees 1-10 … 41-50).
  • Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs — Deathbed addresses of Jacob’s twelve sons to their children, warning against vices and urging virtues (twelve notes).
  • Joseph and Aseneth — Asenath, daughter of an Egyptian priest, renounces idols, is visited by an angel and marries Joseph, then is defended from Pharaoh’s son.
  • Psalms of Solomon — Eighteen Pharisaic psalms reacting to Pompey’s conquest of Jerusalem and hoping for a Davidic Messiah.
  • Testament of Job — Job tells his children of his trial by Satan, his endurance and the heavenly girdles he gave his daughters.
  • Assumption of Moses — Farewell testament of Moses to Joshua, foretelling Israel’s history, the martyr Taxo and God’s final victory.
  • 2 Enoch — Slavonic apocalypse of Enoch ascending through the ten heavens.
  • Ascension of Isaiah — Martyrdom of Isaiah under Manasseh (chs 1–5) and his vision of the ascent through the seven heavens and the descent of the Beloved (chs 6–11).
  • Testament of Abraham — The archangel Michael announces death to Abraham, who tours judgment and is taken to heaven (both recensions).
  • Apocalypse of Moses — Greek version of the Adam and Eve story: Eve’s account of the Fall and the burial of Adam.
  • Life of Adam and Eve — Latin Vita Adae et Evae: penance of Adam and Eve, the fall of Satan, and the deaths of Adam and Eve.
  • 2 Baruch — Apocalypse in which Baruch foresees the fall of Jerusalem, the messianic age and the resurrection, with his epistle to the nine and a half tribes.
  • Apocalypse of Abraham — Jewish apocalypse after 70 AD: Abraham turns from idolatry, ascends to heaven and sees the fall, the Temple destroyed and the end of the age.
  • 3 Baruch — Baruch is guided through five heavens, seeing the tower-builders, the sun-bird, and the merits of the righteous.
  • 4 Baruch — Abimelech sleeps 66 years during the Babylonian exile; Jeremiah and Baruch; return from Babylon and Jeremiah’s martyrdom (Paraleipomena Jeremiou).
  • Odes of Solomon — Early Christian hymnbook of forty-one surviving Syriac odes in the voice of Solomon.
  • Testament of Solomon — Solomon commands demons by a magic ring to build the Temple, and later falls into idolatry.
  • Apocalypse of Sedrach — Sedrach, taken to heaven, pleads with God for sinners.
  • Greek Apocalypse of Ezra — Ezra pleads for sinners in heaven and Hades and is taken at death.
  • On the Life of the Blessed (Zosimus) — The hermit Zosimus visits the blessed descendants of Rechab.
