Gnostic Texts

Gnostic Texts

Nov 25, 20262 min read

Gospels and sayings from the Nag Hammadi library and related codices, with other early gospel texts and fragments in modern translation.

The translations are by Mark M. Mattison and others at gospels.net, dedicated by their translators to the public domain. Entries run in rough order of composition.

  • Gospel of Q — Hypothetical sayings source behind Luke and Matthew.
  • Egerton Gospel — Fragmentary unknown gospel with Johannine-style controversy, a leper healing and a tribute question.
  • Gospel of Peter — Fragmentary passion and resurrection narrative.
  • Gospel of Thomas — Collection of 114 secret sayings of the living Jesus, found in Coptic at Nag Hammadi.
  • Secret Book of James — Revelation of the risen Savior to James and Peter on the cross, parables, and the ascent.
  • Secret Gospel of Mark — Clement’s letter to Theodore quoting an expanded Mark with the raising of a youth at Bethany; authenticity disputed.
  • Gospel of Mary — Dialogue of the risen Savior and Mary Magdalene on sin, the soul’s ascent past the Powers, and Mary’s authority.
  • Gospel of Judas — Jesus’ secret revelation to Judas Iscariot and his betrayal, from Codex Tchacos.
  • Gospel of Truth — Valentinian meditation on Christ as the revealer who dispels Error and ignorance of the Father.
  • Infancy Gospel of James — Mary’s birth and upbringing and the birth of Jesus.
  • Infancy Gospel of Thomas — Miracles and conflicts of the child Jesus.
  • The Stranger’s Book — Codex Tchacos revelation in which Stranger resists Satan and learns the password for the soul’s ascent past the Powers.
  • Gospel of Philip — Valentinian anthology of sayings on names, resurrection, the sacraments and the bridal chamber.
  • Oxyrhynchus Sayings of Jesus (P.Oxy. 5575) — Papyrus fragment of sayings on anxiety and fasting from the world, paralleling Matthew, Luke and Thomas.
