Revival (1700–1800)

Revival (1700–1800)

Oct 25, 20261 min read

The eighteenth century: the Great Awakening in New England and the Evangelical Revival in Britain, and the preaching and divinity that came out of them. All of it is in the public domain.

John Wesley

John Wesley (1703–1791), Anglican cleric, evangelist, and — with his brother Charles — founder of Methodism. The collection gathers his Sermons on Several Occasions (all 141, in the Jackson 1872 numbering) and the Explanatory Notes on the whole Bible, which together are the doctrinal standards of Methodism, along with the Journal, the prose treatises and appeals, and the Poetical Works.

Author index: John Wesley (1,399 notes)

Jonathan Edwards

Jonathan Edwards (1703–1758), pastor at Northampton, missionary at Stockbridge, and the foremost theologian of the Great Awakening. The two-volume Works in the Banner of Truth edition — revised and corrected by Edward Hickman, 1834: Volume 1 opens with Sereno E. Dwight’s Memoirs and the major treatises, Volume 2 holds the sermons, the shorter treatises, the Life and Diary of David Brainerd, and the biblical notes.

Author index: The Works of Jonathan Edwards (589 notes)
