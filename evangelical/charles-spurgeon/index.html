Charles Haddon Spurgeon

Charles Haddon Spurgeon

Sep 05, 20262 min read

Charles Haddon Spurgeon (1834–1892), the “Prince of Preachers,” was the Reformed Baptist pastor of the New Park Street Chapel and then the Metropolitan Tabernacle in London for thirty-eight years. He preached to thousands weekly, founded a pastors’ college and an orphanage, and left one of the largest bodies of published work of any Christian author. All texts here are public domain — the sermons and books from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library, the Treasury of David from The Spurgeon Archive.

“A Bible that’s falling apart usually belongs to someone who isn’t.”

Sermons

The New Park Street Pulpit and Metropolitan Tabernacle Pulpit — Spurgeon’s weekly sermons, 63 volumes, 3,512 sermons in all. Preached 1855–1917.

  • Browse all sermon volumes →

The Treasury of David

  • The Treasury of David — his monumental commentary on all 150 Psalms (150 notes).

Daily Devotionals

  • Morning and Evening — A morning and an evening reading for every day of the year — his best-loved devotional. (366 readings)
  • Faith’s Checkbook — A daily devotional on the promises of God, one for each day. (366 readings)

Pastoral & Ministry

  • Lectures to My Students — His counsel to ministerial students on the preacher’s life and work (3 volumes). (30 notes)
  • The Soul Winner — Addresses on the winning of souls, the church’s great work. (15 notes)
  • Commenting and Commentaries — Two lectures with his catalogue of Bible commentaries. (4 notes)

Books

  • All of Grace — His classic gospel presentation for the anxious enquirer. (20 notes)
  • John Ploughman’s Talk — Homely, proverbial wisdom for plain people — his most popular book. (25 notes)
  • Till He Come — Communion meditations and addresses on fellowship with Christ. (23 notes)
  • Sermons on Proverbs — Forty-four sermons expounding texts from Proverbs. (44 notes)
  • A Puritan Catechism — His revision of the Westminster Shorter Catechism. (2 notes)
