Chapter VIII. On Free Will, in Three Books

  1. While we were still detained at Rome, we sought to learn by debate the origin of evil, and we conducted our debate so that, if possible, long-considered reason might render what, on the strength of divine authority, we believed on this matter, acceptable to our understanding also, so far as we were able, with God’s help, to do so by discussion. And since it was established among us, by sensible and diligent discussion, that evil arises only out of one’s own free will, the three books which came out of that discussion were entitled On Free Will. The second and third books of this treatise I brought to an end in Africa after I had been ordained a presbyter at Hippo Regius, just as I was then able to do it.
