Thus at the present time,19 the bishops Tatwine and Aldwulf preside in the churches of Kent; Ingwald is bishop in the province of the East Saxons. In the province of the East Angles, the bishops are Aldbert and Hadulac;20 in the province of the West Saxons, Daniel and Forthere;21 in the province of the Mercians, Aldwin.22 Among those peoples who dwell beyond the river Severn to the westward,23 Walhstod is bishop; in the province of the Hwiccas, Wilfrid;24 in the province of Lindsey, Bishop Cynibert25 presides; the bishopric of the Isle of Wight26 belongs to Daniel, bishop of the city of Winchester. The province of the South Saxons,27 having now continued some years without a bishop, receives episcopal ministrations from the prelate of the West Saxons. All these provinces, and the other southern provinces, as far as the boundary formed by the river Humber, with their several kings, are subject to King Ethelbald.28

But in the province of the Northumbrians, where King Ceolwulf reigns, four bishops now preside; Wilfrid29 in the church of York, Ethelwald30 in that of Lindisfarne, Acca31 in that of Hagustald, Pecthelm32 in that which is called the White House, which, as the number of the faithful has increased, has lately become an episcopal see, and has him for its first prelate. The Pictish people also at this time are at peace with the English nation, and rejoice in having their part in Catholic peace and truth with the universal Church. The Scots33 that inhabit Britain, content with their own territories, devise no plots nor hostilities against the English nation. The Britons,34 though they, for the most part, as a nation hate and oppose the English nation, and wrongfully, and from wicked lewdness, set themselves against the appointed Easter of the whole Catholic Church; yet, inasmuch as both Divine and human power withstand them, they can in neither purpose prevail as they desire; for though in part they are their own masters, yet part of them are brought under subjection to the English. In these favourable times of peace and calm,35 many of the Northumbrians, as well of the nobility as private persons, laying aside their weapons, and receiving the tonsure, desire rather both for themselves and their children to take upon them monastic vows, than to practise the pursuit of war. What will be the end hereof, the next age will see. This is for the present the state of all Britain; about two hundred and eighty-five years after the coming of the English into Britain, and in the 731st year of our Lord, in Whose kingdom that shall have no end let the earth rejoice; and Britain being one with them in the joy of His faith, let the multitude of isles be glad, and give thanks at the remembrance of His holiness.
