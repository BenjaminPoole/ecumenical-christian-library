Proslogium

1077–10782 min read

Anselm of Canterbury — translated by Sidney Norton Deane (Open Court, 1903; reprint 1926), from CCEL.

Written 1077–78 as Fides quaerens intellectum, “faith seeking understanding.” It opens in prayer and arrives, in Chapters 2–4, at the argument that God is that than which nothing greater can be conceived — the ontological argument, which has been in dispute ever since. Gaunilo’s objection and Anselm’s reply are in In Behalf of the Fool.
