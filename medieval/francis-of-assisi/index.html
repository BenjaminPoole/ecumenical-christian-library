Francis of Assisi

Francis of Assisi

Nov 25, 20264 min readFranciscan

Born: Assisi, Italy, c. 1181. Open in Google Maps

Francis of Assisi (c. 1181–1226), founder of the Friars Minor, who took the Gospel as his Rule and left a small body of writings: counsels, rules, letters, prayers and the Canticle of the Sun. This is the whole collection in Paschal Robinson’s English translation (Philadelphia, 1906), made from the critical Quaracchi edition of 1904, with his introductions and appendix. The *Little Flowers of St. Francis’ (Fioretti) are not included: they were compiled in the fourteenth century and are not by Francis.

Works (20)

  • Admonitions — The twenty-eight Words of Admonition: short counsels on obedience, poverty, humility, charity and the Blessed Sacrament, with Robinson’s note on the manuscripts.
  • Salutation of the Virtues — The salutation to Lady Wisdom, Holy Poverty, Charity and the other virtues, which confound the vices opposed to them.
  • On Reverence for the Lord’s Body — Francis’ instruction to all clerics on the care owed to the Body and Blood of the Lord, the chalices and corporals, and the written Names of God.
  • First Rule of the Friars Minor — The longer, unsealed Rule of the brothers (1209–1221), in twenty-three chapters, which closes with the great prayer and thanksgiving of chapter 23.
  • Second Rule of the Friars Minor — The shorter Rule of twelve chapters, confirmed by Pope Honorius III on 29 November 1223.
  • Fragments from the Rule of the Poor Clares — The two fragments of Francis’ writing to St. Clare and her sisters, preserved in the sixth chapter of her Rule: the form of life and the last wish.
  • Testament — Francis’ Testament of 1226: his conversion among the lepers, his reverence for priests, the Rule and the brothers’ labour and poverty.
  • Regulation for Hermitages — The short rule for friars living in hermitages, three or four together, as mothers and sons, in the lives of Martha and Mary.
  • Letter to All the Faithful — The letter to all Christians: penance, the Eucharist, the love of neighbour, and the death of the miser.
  • Letter to All the Friars — The letter to the General Chapter and all the friars: reverence for the Eucharist and the priesthood, and Francis’ confession and counsel.
  • Letter to a Certain Minister — Counsel to a minister burdened by the offences of his brothers: love them, forgive without limit, and bear it patiently.
  • Letter to the Rulers of the People — The letter to the podestàs, consuls and judges of the world, bidding them remember the day of death and honour the Lord’s Body and Blood.
  • Letter to All the Custodes — The letter to the custodes of the Friars Minor, on the honour due to the Eucharist and the Names of the Lord, and on preaching penance.
  • Letter to Brother Leo — The autograph note to Brother Leo, preserved at Spoleto: “I say to thee, Yes, my son, and as a mother.”
  • Paraphrase of the Lord’s Prayer and Praises — Francis’ line-by-line paraphrase of the Our Father and the Praises (“Holy, Holy, Holy”), with the closing prayer.
  • Salutation of the Blessed Virgin — The salutation to Mary as palace, tabernacle, house, garment and handmaid of the Lord.
  • Prayer to Obtain Divine Love — The short prayer that the fiery and sweet strength of Christ’s love may absorb the soul.
  • Praises of God and Blessing for Brother Leo — The Praises of God and the Blessing for Brother Leo, written on La Verna in 1224 on the parchment given to Leo.
  • Canticle of the Sun — The Canticle of Brother Sun, or the Praises of the Creatures, composed in 1225 at San Damiano.
  • Office of the Passion — The Psalms and antiphons which Francis arranged to recall the Passion of the Lord, for every hour of the liturgical day.

Robinson’s introductions and appendices

  • Introduction to the Writings of Saint Francis — Paschal Robinson’s introduction on the character of the writings, the manuscript collections and the editions.
  • Rules of the Friars Minor — Robinson’s introduction to the two Rules.
  • Introduction to the Letters — Robinson’s introduction to the six letters, and the five attributed letters he excludes.
  • Appendix - Some Lost, Doubtful, and Spurious Writings — Robinson’s appendix on writings attributed to Francis which are lost, doubtful or spurious.
  • Bibliography — Robinson’s bibliography of the sources cited.
