Summa Logicae, Part I

Summa Logicae, Part I

c. 13231 min read

William of Ockham — translated by Paul Vincent Spade (1995), From His Summa of Logic, Part I: Adam of Wodeham’s prologue, Ockham’s prefatory letter and chapters 1–6, 8–13, 26–28, 30–31, 33, 63–66, 70 and 72, with Spade’s summaries of chapters 7, 29 and 32. Licensed CC BY 4.0; from Indiana University ScholarWorks. Changes: format conversion only.

The first part of Ockham’s handbook of logic, on terms: what a term is, mental, spoken and written terms, concrete and abstract names, universals, supposition and the kinds of signification. Spade’s selection runs from Adam Wodeham’s prologue and Ockham’s prefatory letter through chapters 1–13, 26–33, 63–66, 70 and 72, with summaries of the three chapters he does not translate, and his annotations.
