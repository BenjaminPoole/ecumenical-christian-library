Christian writing from the twentieth century onward that is in the public domain.

The Fundamentals

Ninety essays published between 1910 and 1915 by the Testimony Publishing Company of Chicago, funded by the brothers Lyman and Milton Stewart and edited in turn by A. C. Dixon, Louis Meyer, and R. A. Torrey. Written by leading conservative theologians of Britain and North America in defence of orthodox Protestant belief against the higher criticism and Darwinism, and mailed free of charge to pastors and missionaries across the English-speaking world. The work gave its name to the movement later called fundamentalism. Given here in the four-volume arrangement edited by Torrey for the Bible Institute of Los Angeles (1917).

Index: The Fundamentals: A Testimony to the Truth (91 notes)

G. K. Chesterton

English journalist, novelist and apologist (1874–1936), received into the Catholic Church in 1922. Given here: Heretics (1905), his criticism of the leading writers and ideas of his day, Orthodoxy (1908), his account of how he came to believe the Christian faith, The Man Who Was Thursday (1908), his novel of a poet-detective among the anarchists of London, The Ball and the Cross (1909), his novel of an atheist and a Catholic who set out to fight a duel over God and are pursued across England, What’s Wrong with the World (1910), his attack on the modern remedies for social ills and his defence of the home, the family and the common man, St. Francis of Assisi (1923), his life of the founder of the Franciscans, The Everlasting Man (1925), his outline of human history and of Christ’s place in it, written against H. G. Wells, The Catholic Church and Conversion (1926), his account of why people become Catholics and of the mistaken ideas of conversion held by those who do not (written for Hilaire Belloc’s Calvert Series), The Thing (1929, published in America as The Thing: Why I Am a Catholic), his collection of essays defending the Catholic faith against the sceptics, humanists and Protestants of his day, and St. Thomas Aquinas (1933, also known as The Dumb Ox), his popular life of the Dominican philosopher and theologian.
