Chap. XI. — Of the Offices of Christ; and, First, of His Kingly.

Q. 1. How many are the offices of Jesus Christ?

A. Three; first, of a 1King; secondly, of 2a3 4 Priest; thirdly, of a 5Prophet.

Q. 2. Hath he these offices peculiar by nature?

A. No; he only received them for the present dispensation, until the work of redemption be perfected. Ps. cx. 1; Acts ii. 36, x. 42; 1 Cor. xi. 3, xv. 27, 28; Phil. ii. 9; Heb. iii. 2, 6, ii. 7–9.

Q. 3. Wherein doth the kingly office of Christ consist?

A. In a two-fold power; first, his power of ruling in and over his church; secondly, his power of subduing his enemies. Ps. cx. 3–7.

Q. 4. What is his ruling power in and over his people?

A. That supreme authority which,6 7 for their everlasting good, he useth towards them, whereof in general there be two acts; first, 8internal and spiritual, in converting their souls unto him, making them unto himself a willing, obedient, persevering people; secondly, 9eternal and ecclesiastical, in giving perfect laws and rules for their government, as gathered into holy societies under him.

Q. 5. How many are the acts of his kingly power towards his enemies?

A. Two also first, 10internal, by the11 mighty working of his Word, and the spirit of bondage upon their hearts, convincing, amazing, terrifying their consciences, hardening their spirits for ruin; secondly, 12external, in judgements and vengeance, which ofttimes he beginneth in this life, and will continue unto eternity.
