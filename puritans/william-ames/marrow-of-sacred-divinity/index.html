The Marrow of Sacred Divinity

16273 min read

William Ames — the anonymous 1642 English translation of Ames’ Medulla Theologiae (1627); from the Text Creation Partnership’s transcription of that edition, keyed by hand from the page images.

Ames opens with the definition that governs everything after it — divinity is the doctrine of living to God — and builds the whole of theology on it in two books: the first on faith in God, from his essence and decree through creation, the fall, Christ and the application of redemption to the church and sacraments; the second on observance, which is that faith lived. Written in Franeker for students, it was the textbook of New England divinity for a century.

This is the seventeenth-century English Marrow of Sacred Divinity, not the modern translation published as The Marrow of Theology, which is in copyright. Spelling and punctuation are as printed in 1642. A few words the transcribers could not read, and the Greek and Hebrew their transcription does not carry, are marked […].
