Section III — A Discovery of Many False or Insufficient Signs of Grace

  1. Church-priviledges no Sign of Grace
  2. Gifts and Parts in matters of Religion, no sign of Grace
  3. Fully clearing that there may be affections and sweet motions of Heart in holy things, which yet evidence not Grace, nor accompany Salvation
  4. Shewing that from Judgements, Opinions and Disputes, arguments of the truth of Grace cannot be drawn
  5. Declaring what deceipts men lye open to in judging those things to be works of the Spirit, which indeed are not
  6. Manifesting that the greatest Sufferings for Christ are not infallible Evidences of Grace
  7. Shewing that, and whence men have such strong Perswasions of their exact keeping of Gods Law
  8. Declaring that many rest upon a strict way of Religion, which yet cometh not up to, but often is besides the appointment of the word
  9. Zeal and Diligence in false Worship no ground of Comfort
  10. Externall Obedience to the Law of God no sure evidence for Heaven
  11. That a Divine Faith or Perswasion of the Truths in Religion, is not Evidence sure enough of our being in the state of Grace
  12. That every peaceable frame of heart, and perswasion of Gods love, is not a sure Testimony of saving Grace
  13. That outward Successe, Prosperity, and Greatnesse in the world is no true Evidence of Grace
  14. That a mans leaving those gross sins he hath lived in is no Sign of Grace
  15. The Difficulty, and in some sense impossibility of Salvation notwithstanding the easiness which men fancy to themselves thereof

Section IV — The Nature of Sanctifying Grace, under the Notion of the New Birth

  1. Shewing what the New-Birth or Regeneration is
  2. Shewing how ignorant men of great Learning and outward Righteousness in the world, may be of Regeneration
  3. The ground of the necessity of Regeneration is the corruption of mens Nature
  4. Of the unexpressibleness of this new life
  5. Laying open the Counterfeits of a new Birth
  6. Declaring what both by Duty and Priviledge a Son of God is, which he becometh by the New Birth

Section V — The Nature of Grace under the Name of the New Creature

  1. Shewing the Necessity of the New Creature
  2. Shewing that the Production of the New-Creature is from God alone; and what Attributes are conspicuous in the Work
  3. Shewing what the New Creature doth not imply, and to what it is opposed
  4. Concerning the Supernaturality and Excellent Qualities of the New Creature
  5. Answering those Carnall Objections men are apt to make against such a change in themselves
  6. Shewing the Counterfeit of the New-Creature

Section VI — Grace as God’s Workmanship, and Good Works

  1. The New Creature is Gods Workmanship, also its necessity and dignity
  2. That God gives Graces to the most indisposed
  3. Of good works; What to be created unto good works implies, and what works are good
  4. Setting forth divers distinctions, and Axioms to clear the Doctrine of good works

Section VII — The Sanctifying Work of the Spirit

  1. Of the Hearts being established with Grace, and in what sense it is so
  2. Shewing why Grace in the heart, is better then unnecessary Disputes in the head; together with Rules how to manage Disputes and Controversies, without prejudice to Grace
  3. That there is a Work of God upon a mans heart which is called Grace, and why inward holiness is called Grace
  4. Characters and Properties of a Gracious Man
  5. Of the counterfeit of inherent Grace, viz. Natural honesty; and why God hath continued in wicked men the use of Conscience
  6. Shewing more Causes of the Senselesness, Silence and Stupidity of the Consciences of most Men
  7. Shewing how prevalent Natural Conscience hath been in Heathens, in reference to good Laws, Books, Affections, Lives; And how it should stir up Christians to jealousie
  8. Demonstrating That Natural Piety, Honesty and Sobriety in Heathens Is Not Grace
  9. Of Ethicks or Moral Philosophy, that it leads not to Salvation; And that the Moral Vertues of Temperance, Fortitude, Justice, Liberality, &c. are not Grace

Section VIII — The Nature of Converting Grace

  1. To undeceive Men that think, Though they live Wickedly, yet they shall dye Happily. And how it comes to pass that men are so prone to Deceive themselves
  2. That Gods Grace of Conversion, is sometimes vouchsafed to the worst of men
  3. Of the washing and cleansing of a Sinner
  4. Of the Grace of God sanctifying a Sinner
  5. Signes and Characters of Sanctified ones
  6. The counterfeits of Sanctification. That Devotion, in superstitious Customes, or Gods own Ordinances, or being externally in Covenant with God, do not really sanctifie a man
  7. Comfortable Directions to poor Doubting Christians

Section IX — The Work of Grace under the Notion of Conversion

  1. Shewing that the Damnation of Wicked Men is unpleasing to God, and that which he delights not in
  2. Divers Propositions or Considerations introductory to the Doctrine of Conversion
  3. The Impediments and Obstructions of Conversion; And what kinde of persons are most unlikely ever to be converted, not to drive them to despair, but to awaken them out of their Security
  4. The Motives and Grounds, of our turning unto God
  5. Answering some Prejudices and Cavils of ungodly men, against Conversion, or turning unto God
  6. That ungodly Friends and Relations are great Hinderances of ones Conversion, with some helps or Directions to those that are thereby kept in their Sinnes
  7. Some Helps and Encouragements to those that are discouraged, because of the fruitlesness of their Resolutions to Repent, or the hainousness of their Sinnes
  8. Of Gods framing and devising evill of Calamities
  9. Of Afflictions; The difference between Penal-Destructive Calamities, and those that are Medicinal; And how Afflictions are operative to the Conversion of men
  10. Of the time of turning unto God (viz.) presently
  11. That Conversion puts a Man upon leaving his Beloved Sin; The reason of the difference of Darling Sins in men, and the Signs by which a Man may know his beloved Sin
  12. Sheweth that all Persons have need to turn unto God, and sets forth the true acceptable Motives to Repentance
  13. Of the wilful obstinacy that is in some men, with the causes thereof; and that ignorance of ones duty is no excuse
  14. Of turning not onely from sin, but to God; And how many wayes men may do the former, and not the latter; Also, what it is to turn to God
  15. That our turning to God must be with the whole Heart; Wherein Hearty Conversion consists, with the effects of it
  16. Of Tears and Sorrow for Sin; And that they must accompany Conversion
  17. The Nature and Necessity of Godly Sorrow and Weeping for Sinne, as accompanying Conversion
  18. Of the Ministry of the Word as the Means of Conversion, with the other Ends thereof, &c
  19. Why Gods power unto Conversion doth not alwaies go along with his Word though dispensed by a faithful Ministry; and whether corrupt or prophane Ministers may be a means of Conversion

Section X — Grace under the Expression of Taking Away the Heart of Stone

  1. Displayeth Gods Soveraignty and Dominion over the hearts of men
  2. Of the Heart of stone, and what wickednesse that name implies
  3. Of Gods making a true Converts heart tender and flexible for every duty. Also what Heart of flesh implies, with the effects and consequents of it
  4. Holding forth Inducements to get this promised Heart of Flesh; Also shewing the Counterfeit of it

Section XI — Grace under the Notion of God Putting His Spirit Within Us

  1. Of Gods giving his Spirit to a Man, and what that Promise implyes. Also of the Operations and Effects of the Spirit of God where it so comes
  2. That the Work of Grace Is a Deep, Powerful and Inward Affecting of the Whole Man
  3. Of the Order and Method that God takes in our Conversion
  4. That true Principles of Grace within must needs Demonstrate themselves by an Outward Godly Life
  5. God’s Quickening and Assisting Grace, Maintained against Pelagians, Arminians and Papists
  6. Shewing that true Converts make Godlinesse their ordinary Practice; And what to walk in Gods Statutes implies
  7. That Converted Persons are very Carefull and Tender of the Worship of God, observing that and only that which he hath commanded
  8. Of Righteousness and Equity between man and man: And how tender and conscientious a true Convert is therein
  9. The Hammer of Arminianism
  10. The Freenesse of Gods Grace in Conversion displayed and maintained against Arminians and others

Section XII — The Work of Grace under the Title of Vocation

  1. The Nature of Vocation opened in respect of the Efficient and Instrumental Causes of it
  2. Holding forth the Nature of Vocation, or Divine Calling
  3. A further Explanation of the Nature of Effectual calling, by shewing from what, and to what God calls us. As also, the Epithets the Scripture gives it
  4. Contains some choice Properties or Effects that accompany Effectuall Vocation, whereby men may examine and try themselves whether they be so called or no
  5. Stirring up to serious and fixed Meditations upon this Calling, in respect of the manner and time of it, and the persons called
  6. Of true spiritual Wisdom, the Nature and Properties of it, discovering who are the real wise men in the world
  7. Wise men after the flesh characteriz’d, and (by God for the most part) rejected
  8. Reasons why God passeth by Humane VVisdom, with the Difference between it and true spiritual Wisdom; Also the Commendation of Humane Learning, and how necessary it is for a Minister
  9. Of the Consistency of Earthly Greatness and Nobility with Godliness; And yet notwithstanding, how rare it is for such Men to be called and saved
  10. The Gospel Feast, and who are welcome Guests, and who not
  11. That most of those that are called, come short of what is absolutely necessary; And who they be
  12. A Plea for strictness in Religion
  13. Of Election and Reprobation, and of the Fewness and Properties of those that are chosen; Answering the Objections of men, and clearing the Justice of God
  14. More Signs and Effects of Election. And Directions to those that are overwhelmed with Thoughts and Fears whether they are Elected or no
  15. Of Christians walking worthy of their Calling; what to walk worthy implies, and what are the Properties and Actions that will become and grace our holy Calling
  16. Of the Nature, Extent and Example of that Holiness to which God calls a man
  17. Of the Glorious Estate of the Saints in Heaven to which God cals all sinners repenting and believing
  18. The Nature, Possibility, Duty and Means of the Assurance of ones Effectual Calling
  19. The Possibility and Duty of Assurance of our Calling demonstrated: And the Reasons why some thinke a certain Assurance impossible; with Answers to the Objections brought against it
  20. The Advantages the Godly have by Assurance: How it may be known from Presumption; with Directions to the godly that want it
  21. Sheweth in how many respects wicked men (before their effectual Calling) are afar off from God, and consequently miserable
  22. That not all mankinde are called with a saving call
